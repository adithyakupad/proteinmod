# M13 predictive uncertainty and calibration

M13 adds uncertainty diagnostics to existing point predictors, without changing the
GNN/attention architecture or implementing M14/M15. It reuses M12's cohort and original
test IDs, M1 frozen ESM-2, M2 graphs, M4 ridge features and M5 GNN/fusion/attention fits.
All supervised models start fresh; no historical supervised encoder is transferred.

## What the estimates mean

- **Epistemic sensitivity:** 12 within-protein genotype bootstrap ridge fits, or three
  separately initialized/trained neural models. Two independent ensemble/bootstrap
  replicates are evaluated. Dispersion is conditional on these training proteins,
  feature extraction and fitted transforms; it is neither a full posterior nor a
  complete measure of uncertainty about unknown families.
- **Aleatoric uncertainty:** not separately estimated. The processed assay tables do
  not provide independently calibrated replicate variances. Residual error combines
  measurement error, misspecification and epistemic error. The two releases of one
  experimental variant are not independent noise replicates.
- **Structure confidence:** pLDDT/experimental metadata and missingness are inputs,
  not predictive SDs. M12 conservatively masks confidence (see M12's BBC1 erratum).
  M6's AlphaFold inputs retain their original pLDDT semantics. No reinterpretation
  as experimental error or outcome uncertainty is made.
- **OOD diagnostics:** nearest training frozen-ESM feature distance (scaler fitted on
  training only), reference-sequence similarity, family role and structural coverage.
  These are novelty indicators, not calibrated probabilities.

## Outcomes and native-unit eligibility

M12 models train on per-assay training-only midranks. Their predictions cannot be
relabelled as thermodynamic ΔΔG. M13 retains the original DMS scores and scores all 768
variants with within-protein pairwise ordinal discordance. This is an evaluation loss,
not a fitted test CDF, and it supports uncertainty ranking without a shared assay scale.

The existing M6 primary Figure 3 table supplies compatible physical labels for 347
selected M12 variants. They are matched by independent reference, canonical mutation
and complete mutant sequence. Doubles and unavailable/censored/Cys-excluded labels
remain native-ineligible. M12's original target is never replaced. A **secondary affine
regression head** on each model's scalar output fits only training primary labels.
This is a learned predictor with physical target units, not a unit conversion of ranks.
Its extrapolation to new proteins/families can fail, and its fixed-coefficient fitting
uncertainty is not separately included in ensemble dispersion.

Canonical target: ΔΔG_fold = ΔG_fold(mutant) − ΔG_fold(WT), kcal/mol, positive
**destabilizing**. Primary positive-stabilizing ddg values are multiplied by −1 using
the validated M6 adapter. Only this common primary assay/condition task shares residual
widths. Other DMS native scales are not pooled. There is no thermodynamic interval
assessment for the remaining 421 M12 variants and no invented paired function labels.

M6 sanity controls use its original 1,800-genotype single-mutant cohort, separate S1/S2
fits and unchanged test IDs. Only sequence, frozen ESM-site and structural ridges are
used. The new minimal feature loader bypasses an unused old hybrid encoder; its three
feature arrays match the historical M6 arrays exactly. No M6 labels are mixed into
M12 supervision except the explicit same-variant primary-label crosswalk described above.
The separate M6 control never shares fits or calibration with M12.

## Four roles

`configs/m13.json` and the committed preregistration fix all roles/budgets:

| Protocol | Fit | Selection | Calibration | Test |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| G1 | 52 variants/protein | Original 16/protein | 12 native-eligible original training variants/protein | Original 16/protein |
| G2 | Four complete proteins | Original validation protein | Two other complete proteins | Original test protein |
| G3 | Family j+3 | Family j+1 | Family j+2 | Family j |

G2 test/selection proteins have training homologs; calibration proteins come from the
other two families, and also have training-family partners. G3 uses four completely
disjoint families. Calibration selection is salted ID hashing within availability
strata, not target magnitudes or prediction errors. Calibration targets never enter
ridge fitting, target transforms, affine heads or model/epoch selection.

M6 S1 withholds 60 original training variants/protein for calibration. S2 withholds
120 from its sole training protein. Thus S2 calibration is from a seen protein and
cannot establish exchangeability for a new protein. That limitation is reported.

## Interval methods and limitations

Levels are fixed at 80% and 90%. Score quantile uses order statistic
`ceil((n_cal + 1) * nominal_level)`, without clipping the index. If it exceeds n_cal,
the correct result is unbounded; bounds serialize as null with explicit status.

- Independent calibration residual width: |y − prediction|.
- Ensemble-scaled residuals: residual / max(ensemble SD, 0.05 kcal/mol).
- Distance-scaled residuals: residual / max(training distance, 0.05).
- Shuffled-scale control: same procedure, with SD permuted independently within
  protein, role and native-eligibility stratum. This preserves evaluation-cohort
  marginals and uses no target values.
- Validation-residual baseline: uses selection-validation residuals; explicitly
  **not** valid split conformal because model selection used these observations.
- Gaussian ensemble bars: normal quantile × SD, an uncalibrated epistemic-only
  control that ignores residual/noise error.

G1 residual calibration is per protein. G2/G3 pool only compatible primary native
labels. Exchangeability under protein/family shift is violated or unverified: **finite
variant-level intervals are empirical stress tests, not family coverage guarantees**.
One calibration family per G3 fold cannot produce finite 80/90% family-block conformal
intervals. The family-max residual audit correctly returns unbounded quantiles.

Native metrics: coverage, mean/median width, interval score, Spearman(SD, absolute
error), and selective MAE. Ordinal metrics retain all test variants and single/double
breakdowns. Retention 25/50/75/100% is applied within protein; equal protein averages
within family precede equal family averages. Randomized null and constant/distance
rankings are retained. Four families are the replication units; repeat dispersion
is optimization/bootstrap sensitivity, not extra independent families or a CI.

Unbounded intervals contribute trivial coverage but have undefined/infinite overall
width/score, serialized as null; finite-only metrics are explicitly named. They are
never silently discarded to improve apparent calibration. All structural sites map,
so this benchmark does not establish robustness to absent structures.

## API

`proteinmod.uncertainty` exposes bootstrap indices, ensemble summaries, train-only
feature distances, finite-sample quantiles, interval metrics and selective risk.
`predictive_result` combines member predictions and bounds into point/UQ/unit/metadata/
eligibility fields. Ineligible modality positions return nulls, not fabricated values.
`uncertainty_calibration` fits residual scores separately from interval application.
`uncertainty_models` replays selected ridge/GNN states and fits training-only native
heads. Existing M0–M12 APIs and benchmark bytes are unchanged.

## Exact reproduction

Use Python 3.12 and the measured pinned environment; source acquisition is explicit:

```sh
python3.12 -m venv .venv
.venv/bin/python -m pip install -r requirements-m3.txt
.venv/bin/python -m pip install -e '.[dev,esm,m3]'
.venv/bin/python -m proteinmod.family_experiments --acquire
.venv/bin/python -c 'import json; from proteinmod.embeddings import FrozenESM; FrozenESM(json.load(open("configs/m12.json"))["embedding"], local_only=False)'
.venv/bin/python -m proteinmod.family_experiments --prepare
.venv/bin/python -m proteinmod.stability_experiments download
.venv/bin/python -m proteinmod.stability_experiments prepare
```

The last two commands reuse the existing M6 source/graph/token preparation only;
no M5 transfer checkpoint or historical supervised experiment needs rebuilding.
M6 sources are ~36 MB; M12 downloads only its eight selected ProteinGym members.

```sh
LOKY_MAX_CPU_COUNT=4 MPLCONFIGDIR=/tmp/proteinmod-mpl .venv/bin/python -m proteinmod.uncertainty_experiments
LOKY_MAX_CPU_COUNT=4 .venv/bin/python scripts/verify_m13.py
LOKY_MAX_CPU_COUNT=4 .venv/bin/pytest -q
.venv/bin/ruff check .
.venv/bin/ruff format --check .
```

Outputs go to ignored `runs/m13`; raw data, token arrays and model checkpoints remain
ignored caches. The verifier defaults to the committed `benchmarks/m13` evidence and
replays every saved model, native head, calibration quantile, bound and metric from
local caches. For a fresh run, copy the unchanged `PREREGISTRATION.md` and
`preregistration.json` into `runs/m13` and use `--directory runs/m13`.

No binary weights are committed. Source/software revisions, seeds, wall time and CPU
RSS are recorded; frozen ESM token extraction used the existing MPS cache. Exact local
replay is verified. Across different software/hardware, numerical identity is not
promised; pin versions and inspect discrepancies rather than overwrite old artifacts.

The preregistration commit is `32e2c6c`. A corrected native shuffle control was rerun
without changing fits, selection or other interval methods; `control_audit.json`
retains the preliminary comparison and proves all 32,388 point/member/non-shuffle
records were unchanged. The native-control feature-loader simplification is separately
verified against the original M6 features. No held-out-driven hyperparameter search,
noise head, learned structural encoder expansion or LoRA was introduced.

Method context: [Lakshminarayanan et al. (2017)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1612.01474)
introduce deep ensembles as a practical alternative to weight posteriors. The shift
limitations of ordinary conformal calibration are discussed by
[Barber et al. (2023)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.13415). M13 implements ordinary
finite-sample residual quantiles as explicitly qualified empirical diagnostics; it
does not implement their distribution-drift correction methods or claim their bounds.
