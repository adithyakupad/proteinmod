# Interpretation and failure analysis

M5 is a fixed, small pilot on the existing three M4 assays, not a demonstration of unseen-family generalization. The architecture/configuration was committed at `f96a3cd` before evaluation. Subsequent reporting/tests and directory-protection commits did not change the fitted models, training loop, settings or role construction. No test-driven redesign or extended search was performed.

The primary comparisons use exactly the archived M4 structure-matched IDs: 1,731 of 1,800 selected variants (578 amylase, 600 GB1, 553 NucB). G1 has 1,208 training, 261 validation and 262 test variants. G2 holds out each entire protein in turn, using all eligible variants in that protein for test; the validation protein selects epochs and the remaining protein supplies training labels. M4's full sequence-only cohort and its original results remain in `benchmarks/m4`, separately from these matched comparisons. The 69 structural exclusions are label-independent mapping exclusions, not model failures silently removed after prediction. The inherited NucB crop exclusions are recorded separately in the data audit.

## Observed outcome

Bidirectional attention **did not beat weighted or concatenation fusion on G1**: macro Spearman was **0.5156 ± 0.0096**, versus weighted **0.5586 ± 0.0141** and concatenation **0.5563 ± 0.0060**. Structural queries attending to sequence performed best among the predefined new neural models on G1 (**0.5861 ± 0.0028**), but the preserved within-protein one-hot ridge remained stronger (**0.6254**). This is a descriptive comparison, not a reason to select the best architecture using test results.

On G2, bidirectional attention reached **0.1367 ± 0.0179**, above weighted fusion (**0.1068 ± 0.0064**) and concatenation (**0.0928 ± 0.0206**) in this pilot. It did not exceed classical sequence ridge (**0.1428**), handcrafted structural ridge (**0.1669**), the mutation-count baseline (**0.1965**), or clearly surpass the new structure-only control (**0.1404 ± 0.0404**). Sequence queries attending to structure reached **0.1437 ± 0.0081**. The data do not establish a reliable advantage for bidirectional attention.

Bidirectional G2 correlations were **0.2514 amylase**, **0.1056 GB1**, **0.0532 NucB** (means of two seeds). Its G1 values were **0.5841**, **0.6848**, **0.2778**. Weighted fusion's G2 NucB correlation was **−0.0135**; structural→sequence attention was **−0.0222**. NucB transfer remains weak despite adequate mapped geometry. Ordinal labels, assay shift, tiny protein diversity and training on a different protein are plausible contributors, not experimentally isolated causes.

G1 bidirectional train/validation/test Spearman was **0.6885/0.6550/0.5059** for seed 0 and **0.7327/0.6303/0.5252** for seed 1. G2 training correlations **0.54–0.93** contrasted with validation **−0.05–0.29**; these use different proteins and indicate poor transfer rather than a clean estimate of within-distribution overfitting. Selected G2 epochs varied **3–17**. Both-direction attention added parameters (14,113 versus weighted fusion 11,234) without a robust performance advantage. These observations support keeping simpler baselines and improving cross-protein evidence before expanding model complexity.

Normalized bidirectional G1 attention entropy was **0.979–0.998** across protein/direction/seed averages (1 is uniform). Attention was diffuse rather than sharply localized. That is consistent with limited specialization in this small regime; entropy alone cannot establish representation collapse or biological irrelevance. `runs.json` retains per-direction entropy/peak sums and query counts for independent inspection. All real matched graphs contained valid keys; fully masked-key behavior is covered by synthetic tests, not claimed as an observed biological case.

For a population-controlled G1→G2 comparison, `summary.json` additionally restricts G2 to exactly G1 test IDs. On this same population, bidirectional G2 is **0.0678 ± 0.0137**, below weighted fusion **0.1012 ± 0.0170**; the apparent bidirectional advantage over weighted fusion in primary G2 therefore does not persist under this population restriction. Structural→sequence attention falls to **0.0325 ± 0.0339**. Primary G2 results above use all structurally valid test-protein variants, as specified by M4; the primary G1/G2 difference therefore also reflects population differences. Mutation-burden and reference-B-factor strata retain individual protein/seed sizes and undefined values. Small strata and multiple descriptive comparisons preclude strong conclusions.

56 fits generated **27,902 predictions**, with fixed validation-only epoch selection. Total benchmark elapsed time was **469.70 s**; peak process RSS **1.61 GiB**. Frozen residue extraction took **56.51 s** across the three proteins, separate from training. Cached embedding generation and exact repeat checks are recorded in the audit/verification files; original M4 baselines were reused verbatim after exact role-ID checks rather than needlessly retrained.

## Scope and uncertainty

There are only three proteins, one assay per protein, and two neural seeds. SD across two seeds is not a confidence interval. Pretraining overlap and evolutionary family separation have not been established. Aggregated rank correlations do not imply common physical units across assays. NucB has four ordinal activity levels; ties restrict the information available. The G2 target calibration is transferred from a different protein; no held-out protein label is used to fit or scale targets. Raw cross-assay RMSE would be misleading, so G2 RMSE is undefined. G1 per-assay RMSE uses only its training-label empirical CDF as calibration, with evaluation labels passed through that fixed transform.

All variants describe changes in a reference graph. Missing/mismatched residue nodes are absent before message passing, and variants with mutations at those nodes are excluded consistently for every matched model. Full-length sequence keys can still include structurally unmapped non-mutated positions. Experimental B factors are not pLDDT or calibrated correctness probabilities; `structure_strata.csv` uses within-structure reference-residue median B factors only as a descriptive quality proxy, never for selecting models. `coverage.json` reports reference coverage and variant exclusions. Secondary-structure and confidence descriptor missingness is inherited explicitly from M2/M3. Geometry is not experimentally observed mutant rearrangement.

The seven new neural models share dimensionality, GNN depth, optimizer, epoch budget and role construction, but parameter counts and random initialization streams differ. Comparing against both new neural single-modality controls and immutable M4 models avoids attributing every gain to attention; M4's original GNN uses a different normalization/readout and training schedule. No significance claim or universal ranking is justified by this pilot.

## Reproduction and evidence

From repository root, install the measured environment using `python -m pip install -r requirements-m3.txt` and `python -m pip install -e '.[dev,m3,esm]'`. Obtain the M4 source datasets, reference structures and original pooled caches as described in `docs/m4_protocol.md`. Then run the three commands in `docs/m5_protocol.md`: M5 prepare, run, and report. On a fresh machine, the pinned ESM checkpoint must first be acquired with the M1/M4 preparation workflow; M5 preparation deliberately uses local files only.

Frozen residue extraction used MPS float32 and the pinned ESM-2 checkpoint. Training used deterministic CPU float32 and four threads. Cached residue matrices occupy approximately 649 MiB and remain ignored, along with selected checkpoints. No large binary feature/model artifacts are committed. `audit.json` records token-cache contracts, encoder revisions/versions, ordered IDs, graph provenance and checksums. `manifest.json` records environment, runtime, source hashes and peak process RSS. Runtime is descriptive local elapsed time, not an isolated hardware benchmark; verification processes also ran locally.

Offline tests cover Q/K/V directions and dimensions, fully masked softmax, masked keys and padded queries, WT, residue correspondence, mutation-order and graph-node permutation invariance, finite gradients through both attention directions/GNN, cache integrity, protected historical paths, and test-label poisoning. Historical M3 and M4 checkpoint replays verify the encoder refactor's predictions are exactly unchanged. One independent G1 bidirectional seed-0 fit checks exact selected checkpoint tensors, epoch selection and predictions. Reproducibility across different hardware or regenerated CPU versus MPS embeddings is not claimed to be bit-identical.

M6 multitask/stability heads, uncertainty decomposition, LoRA/fine-tuning and generative design are excluded. Attention weights are descriptive model internals, not mechanistic or causal evidence.
